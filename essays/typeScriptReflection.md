Although it's been a while since I've touched any code, and this is my first time learning about JavaScript or TypeScripting, it was a surprisingly pleasant experience. I think I was a bit worried initally since it's been some time since I've thought about variables or functions, but much of learning JavaScript was relearning familiar material with a twist. The ‘var’, ‘let’, and ‘const’ keywords were new to me but easy to understand, and the difference between strict and loose equality might get a bit confusing, but overall it's similar to when I learned Java and C.

And TypeScript was even better; I especially enjoy the intuitive type inference, even if I prefer explicit typing for brevity. I'm still trying to wrap my head around ES6, just because it's a different and new syntax than I'm used, but I can understand the value of arrow functions and named imports. Especially named imports; whenever I'm programming, I sometimes wanted to be specific in what I'm importing into my program, and now I can be explicit with the need of comments.

## Athletic Software Engineering and the Practice WODs

Athletic software engineering does feel stress-inducing, but that's the point, and it isn't without it's benefits. I personally feel more engaged and focused when I'm under the pressure of a hypothetical time limit. While I do end up wasting some of that time trying to test my code or organize my lines for clarity, doing it over and over builds up an understanding in my head, where I'm able to registar and make sense of the problem and solve it faster, and better. 

And I'll admit, sometimes I spend some of my time looking through my notes, trying to remind myself what a function looks like again, but it's all part of the process of reviewing and remembering that makes learning easier. Or at the very least, it'll make solutions instinctively recognizable, cutting down on time. The better I can recall, the better I can implement in my work.

## My Current Perspective with Typescript

Even if most of the content of learning JavaScript and TypeScript was relearning variables and function, some of it was new, and overall it felt a deluge of information was cramming itself into my head. I was initally overwhelmed when I say over 100 lessons for JavaScript, relief when the first half was familiar territory, and tentative bemusement when confronted with ES6. And all 3 of those feelings all over again when learning TypeScript. Personally, it's an odd style of learning, but comprehensible. It'll definetly take some getting used to.

I think, besides the slight confusion or the tiniest amount of stress, I'm enjoying learning TypeScript. But it's only been 2 weeks since the start of the course, so there'll always be room for improvement. I imagine that a few weeks from now, I'll be a bit more comfortable with working in TypeScript, and confidence is key for good performance and a solid positive mentality.
