## I. Introduction

Personally, I feel that AI can be a very risky tool to use for education purposes, in that it can be a helpful assistance in studying or note-taking but also as an unnecessary crutch to cheat with. But throughout this course, I've seen how beneficial AI can be, especially in terms of programming. AI assistants can provide instant feedback and evaluation for optimization, and it also helps with explaining concepts or bugs and how to fix or improve the pre-existing code. Of course, there's always the reality that these kinds of tools can be abused, used to bypass the cognitive effort it takes to do work or solve errors. 

Which is why I try to use it sparingly in my work — AI is useful, yes, but it also cannot completely replicate the human experience of learning and applying experience. Most of the time, I would attempt to do my work without any AI assistance, with the occasional Google search here and there to recap earlier concepts or documentation. And that usually runs the risk of running into Google AI overview, which I find to be an okay summary, and a good list of references to search through. And when I'm having a particularly hard time with completing a task or understanding a concept with no other options, I would consult with Google Gemini for troubleshooting or clarification.

## II. Personal Experience with AI:

1. **Experience quizzes e.g. E18** — Sometimes I would use AI to troubleshoot my code if something looked off or there was a problem I didn't anticipate. But most of the time I let myself find the answer on my own because what good is a learning experience if you don't learn from it? Although, I believe there was a time where I needed help writing code for a recursive function, but likely nothing else.

2. **Practice quizzes** — I think I did okay on the practice quizzes and I didn't usually need AI to help much, other than troubleshoot issues, but other than that, I mostly spent my time focusing on making my own code or looking up similar documentation online.

3. **Quizzes** — Again, I would use AI to troubleshoot my code, but only nearing the end of the timer and when I've written all that I can or know. Most of the time, the AI assistant would give slight solutions to optimize my code better, but occasionally I wouldn't need it to solve my problems much. Sometimes, however, I found it to be good for explaining where I went wrong if there were such issues, so that I can learn from my mistakes.

4. **Essays** — I've never felt the need to use AI assistance nor have used AI when writing essays, as it just feels unnecessary. Especially when it's an reflection of my experience, which are moments that feel most appropriate to write the thoughts in my head into readable text. Moments like that are easy to write about, just difficult to organize, but that too is an appropriate feeling.

5. **Final project** — There are many times where I've utilized AI for the Final Project, mostly for troubleshooting my code, trying to find solutions to problems I wasn't so sure of. I found it to be a reliable source of aid when it came to deployment and working with Nextjs.

6. **Learning a concept / tutorial** — If I couldn't look up a good summary or a concept or a tutorial I could understand easily, I would occasionally use AI to simplify things for me. Design Patterns was one such concept that warranted a deeper inspection with AI, and it certainly made things a little more uncomplicated.

7. **Answering a question in class or in Discord** — I've never used AI for answering questions in class or on Discord: not much questions asked in general to warrant the need for AI assistance and any questions that were asked wouldn't warrant the need for AI assistance.

8. **Asking or answering a smart-question** — As the same as there previous response, I never needed AI to ask or answer a smart-question. Unless asking smart-questions to AI counts, then most of the time I would; I'd prefer clarity over a simple solution to a problem.

9. **Coding example e.g. “give an example of using Underscore .pluck”** — I would sometimes ask AI to give example of concepts, mostly to understand how some code worked or interacted with each other. I did use AI a few times when working on assignments that required the [Nextjs Application Template](https://github.com/ics-software-engineering/nextjs-application-template), since much other the content was so very foreign to me.

10. **Explaining code** — AI is a good tool for explaining code, at least to me. When I became stuck on an error that didn't make sense or problems that seemed to come from nowhere, I'd ask AI to explain the problem, and it would usually tell me that it was simple issue of missing lines or misspelled words. Things I wouldn't have caught if I didn't have another set of (mechanical) eyes to beta test for me. 

11. **Writing code** — I would occasionally require AI to assist in writing code, especially if whatever I wrote came out wrong or wasn't at all what was needed. Most in the beginning of the course, when I wasn't quite used to TypeScript and working in these new code editors.

12. **Documenting code** — I didn't really need AI to help me document my code, most of the time I didn't really need documentation; I supposed the closest I got to such a thing would be GitHub's Copilot giving me generated titles and descriptions for my commits, but even then I already could tell what was modified based off of the history of changes.`

13. **Quality assurance e.g. “What’s wrong with this code <code here>” or “Fix the ESLint errors in <code here>”** — Most of my AI usage came from troubleshooting errors in my code and finding ways to fix them. The promptness and directness of AI really helps in these kind of scenarios, especially if I felt burnt out from attempting to solve the problem myself.

14. **Other uses in ICS 314 not listed** — I've never really used AI beyond the course element above, as I can't really think of any other uses in ICS 314 I'd use it in. I mostly concentrated my AI usage to explaining and writing code, and quality assurance.

## III. Impact on Learning and Understanding:

At first, I felt that using AI was hindering my learning experience — while it's promptness is very advantageous, it's also a bit addicting to have the solutions in the palm of your hand just like that. I'd be easy to complete every task in your way without issues, but that can mess with your head, make you sloppy and unprepared to deal with issues head-on without AI. It's why I try not to use it unless I come across a particularly difficult and challenging predicament. I feel like AI usage can enhance the learning experience and understanding of software engineering concepts, but not unless it's used sparingly and in moderation — it's a tool and not a cure-all, and it should be considered as such. It's great for the first few weeks of this course and getting to know new concepts, but then I started using it less and less over time due because I felt confident that I could do things myself.

## IV. Practical Applications:

I suppose AI does have a few practical and beneficial application, such as software algorithms that handle data entry or services that offer suggestions and tailored content. But there also a few snags to deal with, like the moral implications of collecting user data and analyzing behavior patterns to predict user preferences. But I won't deny that AI is efficient and can help streamline code development, but I also believe that AI has a great ways to go before it can be reliable dependable. Otherwise, I think AI is okay for what it does now — although I still have a personal gripe with generative AI, especially when it's utilized to create 'creative' works. Art is a human creation, and there's simply nothing human about using AI to generate 'art'.

## V. Challenges and Opportunities

AI can give easy and simple solutions, but sometimes those solutions aren't always applicable to my situation. It helped to be more specific about what my exact situation was, but sometimes it wasn't always worth the hassle. It's tempting to rely on AI for everything, but that isn't always the case; regardless, instead of solving problems, I found AI to be most useful in a reliable way were explaining concepts, as it isn't really that difficult to group up a few sources in which summaries exist.

<hr>

## VI. Comparative Analysis

Traditional teaching methods like the ones in ICS 314, like the instructor videos and WOD exercises, were a good source of learning during this course, while AI mostly acted as secondary source of learning. The videos were a very great amount of help when it came to understanding what to do for certain situations, but other than searching through third-party sources online, it scarcely helped me with understanding *why* certain lines of code worked while others didn't. Which is where AI came in handy for — it felt nice to be able to bounce ideas off of something that could offer similar solutions, even if it wasn't a person. It helps that code is such an integral part of AI that it just feels natural that AI would be the perfect assistant to support code optimization. But a consequence of having an online, asynchronous course was that there wasn't much exchanges between individuals; although I wouldn't count AI as an individual, it does act close enough to one that it was more beneficial than detrimental. I still think, however, that a more human, face-to-face, and personal interaction still beats asking AI for help in terms of teaching and education.

<hr>

## VII. Future Considerations

I'll reiterate here again, I do think AI has a good future in software engineering education, and education in general, but there are many hurdles that need to addressed first. I know it's a current problem in schools where student rely on AI so much that it's degrading their cognitive abilities — I imaging it really tickles the dopamine section of the brain to be able to get easy answers by typing a few words. And it can be difficult facilitating AI use for homework or quizzes or even important tests. But when it comes to software engineering itself, I do think that a self-disciplined student's education would be greatly supported by AI assistance, in terms of both efficiency in both learning to and actually programming.

## VIII. Conclusion

While I try not to use it much myself, I understand why AI usage is not only accept by encouraged in this course. I've used AI before in my previous ICS classes, but I've never really thought much about is a tool than how I feel about it now. My approach to AI is that it can be used as a useful, but not much else — it really shouldn't be considered as a reliable solution even if it does seem like such and it would be very beneficial. I would still approve of AI usage for learning or brainstorming, whether for this course or for other academic subjects — certainly not Creative Writing, or similar topics — since it could be considered a good boon in terms of availability and efficiency. Just as long as it actually helps with the usual hands-on approach that comes with learning how to build software or is used by a rational and well-meaning student for the purposes of becoming more proficient in coding rather than quick with completed assignments.
